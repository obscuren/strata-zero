# The city and the world

The city is five strata deep, from the undercity at the bottom to the Net over everything, and past its edge lies a whole country to cross.

| Stratum | What's there |
|---|---|
| Undercity | tunnels, sewage, the dark |
| Street | where people live and work |
| Skyways | walkways and bridges between towers |
| Heights | rooftops and the tops of towers |
| Net | the city's systems, over it all |

The Street is the busy one. The people who walk the city walk there, and it is the only stratum that runs on past the city's edge. The Net has a chapter of its own.

## Going up and down

`>` goes up and `<` goes down. Stand on the way between strata and press one, or press `space` on it for a window with both.

- A **manhole** joins the Undercity and the Street.
- **Stairs** join the Street and the Skyways.
- A **fire ladder** on the side of a tall building joins the Skyways and the Heights.

Every district has a manhole and a set of stairs open to anyone. When nothing leads the way you pressed, the log says so.

## Buildings

Most buildings are solid on the map; you go in through their doors (`▯`). Bars, markets, shops and arcades are open to the street, and you can walk straight in.

Inside, floors are named as a lift would: `B1` under the street and `B2` under that, `L` at street level, then `2F`, `3F` and up. A door from the Street opens onto `L`, a bridge from the Skyways onto its floor, a roof door from the Heights onto the top floor, and a door from an Undercity tunnel onto `B1`. Stairs take you to the next floor they stop at. A building of three floors or more has a lift; `<`, `>` or `space` at the lift asks which floor, with the one you're tracking marked `⌖`.

Doors to flats and offices are often locked. A keycard, or a code you've learned, opens them; your journal keeps every code you know. Your own door always opens for you.

### Hotels and offices

Hotels and offices are built floor by floor, and no two floors are quite the same. A hotel has a car park at `B1` and a service floor at `B2`, its lobby at `L`, guest floors above and a penthouse near the top. An office has open-plan floors over its lobby, an executive floor near the top and a server room under the street. Rooms are numbered by floor: `1204` on `12F`, and `B1-04` under the street.

Each has a Net of its own, with a level for each floor, behind a firewall. A terminal set in a wall (`◇`) or the switch box of a line of turnstiles (`▪`) is a way onto it from the floor (The Net).

There is more than one way between floors. A hotel has a guest lift and a service lift, a grand stair up from the lobby, a service stair and a fire stair. An office has two banks of lifts, a goods lift down to its basement, a stair and a fire stair.

- A lift lists only the floors it stops at. If yours doesn't go where you want, another lift or a stair might.
- A fire stair lets you on at every floor but off only at its top and bottom.

### Zones and keycards

A hotel or office is split into zones, from the public lobby to the staff's rooms and the secure ones deeper in. The doors between zones are locked.

- A door with a **card reader** is drawn in orange. A **zone keycard** opens the card doors and lifts of its zone and of every zone before it, so a secure card opens the staff doors too. Staff keep theirs on a lanyard, and you won't get one off them. The Net opens card doors too: crack a door's lock on the building's own Net and that door opens for an hour, not every door in the building (The Net). An alarm ends that: those doors say locked down, and only a card, or a fresh crack on the door's lock made since the alarm went off, opens them until it's over.
- Deeper doors have a keypad instead, worked as a flat's is: a code, the Keyjack or the Net opens it.
- Getting out is free. Every zone door opens with no key from the deeper side, and a locked room opens from inside, so you can always leave a zone without a key, even once a crack runs out.
- A lift can ask for a card at a stop. On its panel a floor you can't reach is greyed with the card it wants. It asks only where you get off: riding away from a carded floor takes nothing. Spoof the lift's node on the building's Net and it asks for no card at any floor it serves.

An office keeps its lifts and stairs behind the turnstiles in its lobby, for staff only. Its turnstiles have keypads: a code or the Keyjack gets you through, as do a card and the Net (the line's gate switch), or find another way in. Out through them is free.

A hotel's or office's main street door is always open. Its other doors, from the Street, the Skyways, the roof or the Undercity, may open past the public rooms. Such a door is locked, with a keypad outside: a code, the Keyjack, a keycard for its zone or the Net opens it. From inside it always opens.

### Vents and ducts

Hotels and offices have crawls: vents and ducts behind a grille (`☰`), running between floors and in from the roof or a tunnel outside. They go round the locks, but a grille takes TECH 6 to open. With it, `<` and `>` on a grille take you along the crawl to the next floor it comes out on, and `space` on the roof or tunnel tile where one starts offers to crawl in. Without it, `space` beside a grille shows what it takes.

### Keypads

A locked flat has a keypad on its door, and so do the deeper doors of hotels and offices, their locked rooms, an office's turnstiles and their locked doors in: `space` next to it and `i Interact: Keypad` brings it up. Type the four digits and press `V`; `X` clears them, and `V` with nothing typed keys in the code your journal holds. A right code reads `OPEN` and goes into your journal. Three wrong codes lock the pad for half an hour, and it counts down `LOCKED` on its glass.

### The Keyjack

Every runner carries a **Keyjack**, a lock cracker on a lead. While you carry one, the pad shows `k  clip the keyjack` under itself: press `k` and it clips on to the pad's right, then works the code out with you a digit at a time. A locked pad won't take it.

A marker sweeps the bar on its screen. Press `space` while the marker sits in the lit zone. A hit keys that digit into the pad, and the next beat starts with a narrower zone. The fourth hit opens the door, and the code goes into your journal. `esc` pulls the Keyjack off; the pad stays up, with nothing typed.

A miss is a **fault**: the bar flashes `ERR`, a fault light comes on and the beat starts again somewhere else on the bar. Faults stay on the door. Pulling the Keyjack off and clipping it on again starts the beats over, not the faults; they clear when the door opens or when its lockout ends. The last fault, the third with a stock fuse, locks the pad for half an hour, the same as three wrong codes, and blows the Keyjack's fuse. The screen reads `FUSE  BLOWN` and the fuse is gone.

The Keyjack runs on the fuse in its socket. When none you carry has a fuse in, the line under the pad greys to `keyjack · no fuse` and `k` only tells you so. Fit a spare from the Keyjack's blueprint (The deck covers inspecting and modding). Better fuses help: a ceramic fuse sits through one more fault, a slow-blow fuse slows the marker, and a wideband fuse makes every zone a cell wider. With the WRENCH category you can build Keyjacks and fuses at the workbench.

A lock's maker sets its rating, shown on the Keyjack's screen beside the beat. Street locks are `R1`, municipal and neon ones `R2`, megacorp ones `R3`. The higher the rating, the narrower the zones.

What you've learned counts too. The **Lockpick** skill (GHOST) makes every zone a cell wider, and the **Skeleton key** talent allows one more fault. Both add to what the fuse does.

## Light and dark

Lamps come on at six in the evening and go off at six in the morning; dawn and dusk take two hours each. In the dark you see less: about fourteen tiles on the street, eight in the undercity. Lit places can be seen from much further away. Cameras need light too: in the dark one only sees you if you are within a few tiles of it.

## Past the city's edge

Walk off the edge of the Street and you are out on open ground, with the next town or city on the same map ahead of you. Water too deep to wade and slopes too steep to climb stop you. You can't leave things lying around out there.

### The overworld

`W` on the open street or open ground lifts you up to the overworld: the whole country in one map, with its cities, towns, roads and hills. Walk a tile at a time with the movement keys, and press `enter` to go down onto the tile you stand on. Step onto a city or a town and you are in it.

Out here every step takes time: a tile of flat ground is most of a minute, hills twice that, mountains four times, and a river with no road crossing it twice. Peaks, lakes and the sea stop you. The world holds nine cities. Your home city's story waits for you while you're away, and a call that couldn't reach you rings again when you come back. Cities you've left carry on with their day without you: come back and their people are where the hour has taken them.

## People

The city's people keep their own hours: shopkeepers work the day, noodle cooks and barkeeps the night, fixers and ripperdocs from six in the evening until four in the morning. `space` next to someone shows who they are and what they're doing, and `t` talks to them. A fixer has work to offer as well (Fixers, jobs and the story).
