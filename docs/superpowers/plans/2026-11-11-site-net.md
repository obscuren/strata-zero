# A Site's Own Net Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Every hotel and office (and a metro station laid from the console) gets a Net of its own, worked out from its plan and its mission copy's layer: a construct of one level per kept floor behind a firewall, where each node works its own device, entered from a floor, through the firewall or by a backdoor, and drawn as the open tower (#95).

**Architecture:**
- citygen works the site's Net out, never by chance: `venue::devices` places the terminals and the gate switches by a pass of their own; `citygen::sitenet` makes the node table (its riser, hubs, server, vault and air gap), the firewall's locks (from the city's Net map, so old cities get them) and the construct: one `Construct` whose grid holds every kept level, one under another, each its floor tile for tile, with `levels` (`NetLevel`) and `devices` (`NetDevice`) saying where each level and node stands.
- strata-zero's `sitenet` module holds the rules at play. A node is named `SiteNode { building, level, at }` (`HackTarget::Site`; `NetNode::Site` in holds and the feed), so a lock, a lift, a turnstile line opens to a hack on its own node. The firewall's broken locks are `NetState.firewalls`. The player on a site's Net is at `Place::SiteNet { building, level }`, which the engine treats as a construct of its building.
- The map draws a site's construct as look D (`render/netlook/tower.rs`) with its panel; the city's Net carries the building's locks; HELIX, floor cameras, the dev start, the shots, saving notes and the manual follow.

**Tech Stack:** Rust workspace (`crates/citygen`, `crates/game`, `crates/strata-zero`); serde, rmp-serde (`to_vec_named`) for saves; ratatui; `rand_chacha::ChaCha8Rng` seeded through `citygen::rng::hash64`; RON for `flavor/sites.ron`.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-11-site-net-design.md`. The look is look D in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-11-site-net-mock/site-net.html`, drawn by `gen.py` (`look_d`, `d_open`, `d_strip`, `d_node`, `side_head`, `grid_strip`): read it for colours and glyphs.

## Rulings

Where the spec leaves a choice to the builder, or the code forces one, this plan decides:

1. **Ruling:** A site's construct is one `Construct` whose grid holds its kept levels one under another, the highest at the top, each at x 0 at its floor's own size, `BAND_GAP` (2) rows of Net wall between them. `Construct.levels: Vec<NetLevel>` and `Construct.devices: Vec<NetDevice>` (both `#[serde(default)]`, empty in every other construct) say where each level and node stands. It is saved whole, as constructs are, so its ICE, where each piece stands and what's lifted are saved with each level's band (the spec's "saves per level"). The engine keeps working on it unchanged: one `Vec<Ice>` per building in `NetState.ice`, `HackTarget::Guard { building, index }` across all its levels.
2. **Ruling:** A node is `SiteNode { building, level, at }`, `at` being the device's own tile on its floor (a door's tile, a lift's landing, a terminal's or switch's wall tile, a camera's, turret's, container's or vault's spot, a hub's or the server's floor tile). A firewall lock is `SiteNode::firewall(b, i)`: level `SiteNode::FIREWALL` (`i32::MIN`), at `(i, 0)`, the same node from the grid and from inside. Node order: levels lowest first; then Hub, Server, DataVault, Firewall, Terminal, GateSwitch, DoorLock, LiftCtrl, Camera, Turret, Container; then `(at.y, at.x)`.
3. **Ruling:** New `NodeKind`s join the enum at its end: `GateSwitch`, `Turret`, `Container`, `Hub`, `Firewall`. A crack applies to every node kind but a hub; a spoof also applies to a gate switch; nothing applies to a hub.
4. **Ruling:** A node's tile on the Net is its device's tile, but a device set in a wall (a terminal, a gate switch) stands on the floor tile in front of it, and a firewall lock in G's outer wall. Exits to the city are Net wall, carrying their lock's node when a way-in lock with the Net way stands there; a door with no lock is Net floor; one with a Net lock is Net floor carrying its node; one whose lock has no Net way is Net wall; a turnstile is Net floor that stops you until its switch is held. Landings, stairs, escalators, ladders and track are Net floor. Light: one glow by security, as today's constructs.
5. **Ruling:** Terminals are set in a wall of their room, facing a free floor tile; they are not drawn into floors (floors saved before stay right): the floor map draws them from the pass. "Free": `Terrain::Floor`, no feature, not in the level's `reserved`, not a room's spot, not a door's doorstep. Data: each type's `terminals: [...]` names the rooms with a desk terminal; by rule G's public kiosk stands in the room holding the main street door's doorstep (else G's first zone-0 room), and a gapped level's terminal in the room of the level's lowest zone that holds a link's landing (else the room of that zone nearest one). Each room's pick comes from `ChaCha8Rng::seed_from_u64(hash64(hash64(hash64(layout.seed, TERMINAL_SALT), level as i64 as u64), room as u64))`.
6. **Ruling:** A gate switch is set in the wall tile just before the line's first turnstile along the line, facing the side of its door's shallower zone; when that front tile isn't free, the wall tile just past its last turnstile. A line's turnstiles aren't nodes of their own: the switch works them all.
7. **Ruling:** The riser follows the lift serving the most kept levels (ties: link order); with no lift, the link of any kind serving the most; with no link, none (G's hub alone). Its shaft on a level is its stop there, else its stop on the nearest level it serves. A hub is the first free floor tile of: west of the bank, east of its second tile, under the bank (two tiles), over it (two tiles); else the free floor tile nearest the shaft (manhattan, then y, x). The server is the first free floor tile 4-beside G's hub, then 8-beside, in (y, x) order.
8. **Ruling:** The data vault is a copy's objective spot when the objective's room is of kind `Server`; in the plain building at 230 or more, the first `Server` room (levels lowest first, rooms in order) at its first spot, else its centre.
9. **Ruling:** ICE "every other kept level" is the kept levels other than G at odd places in the list lowest first. The white at G's hub posts on the free tile nearest the hub (never the hub or the server). A patrol walks from beside its level's hub through the centre tile of each of the level's rooms that isn't private, in room order, by shortest 4-step paths over the level's open Net tiles; a level with fewer than 2 such rooms walks from beside its hub to its farthest open tile. Black at 230 and up stands beside the vault, no beat. `Construct::refresh_ice` puts a node's feature back when ICE steps off it.
10. **Ruling:** In a site's construct ICE sees you on any open tile in its row or column (the floor stands in for the spine); only your level's ICE takes turns with you and counts as awake.
11. **Ruling:** The firewall's locks on the city's Net: lock 0 is the building's gate tile; each next one is the open-grid tile (`NetGrid`, outside the footprint, 4-beside its border, no node on it) farthest by Chebyshev distance from the locks placed (ties: y, x); with too few, the footprint's own non-corner border tiles the same way. Inside, lock i stands on G's frame wall at the non-corner, non-door tile nearest its projection `((x - fp.x) * w / fp.w, (y - fp.y) * h / fp.h)`, taken tiles skipped; lock 0's is the construct's gate (`NetGate`, its `entry`).
12. **Ruling:** A broken lock is `(lock, until)` in `NetState.firewalls[b]`, `until` = now + `HACK_MINUTES`; an alarm clears the building's. Through the firewall you land on G's hub; out, by stepping onto the gate inside.
13. **Ruling (old saves):** a save standing in a site's old construct carries on there until you step out of it (the spec's first way). The site's construct replaces an old one when first made, dropping its ICE states and its guard hacks. A hack or hold from before keeps naming its city node; a street door's city lock opens that door only.
14. **Ruling:** A site is a building whose plan is a venue: the session's plan when it holds one (an old save's hotel kept on the classic builder is no site and keeps its old construct), else a kind a venue type builds.
15. **Ruling:** Root on a site is `Tier::Admin` (a cracked server, or a backdoor's root). With it, `door release` spoofs every door lock and gate switch, `lift answer` every lift and `cam loop` blinds every camera on the riser's levels; `alarm off` is as today. ssh's refusal reads `ssh: {host}: FIREWALL · 3 LOCKS` and comes before a key is spent.
16. **Ruling:** A floor camera faces away from the first wall beside its spot in `Dir::CARDINAL` order; with none, toward the nearest Net lock on its level; with none, south. Its cone runs from its spot. Floor cameras warm the trace once per building per minute, as the street's do.
17. **Ruling:** A crack landing on a turret or a container is a hack (its hour, dropped by an alarm), shown under HELD as cracked; only cameras and terminals are holds with verbs.
18. **Ruling:** `<` and `>` on a hub are calm only; you land on the next level's hub, beside it when ICE stands there.
19. **Ruling:** You jack in on a floor beside (Chebyshev 1) a terminal's or a switch's wall tile; on the floor its only choice is `j Jack in`. The body line: `{level} · at the {device}`; who's near: `{n} guards on {level} · nearest {d} tiles`, or `nobody on {level}`.
20. **Ruling:** The panel's job line is the live copy's objective, `{objective name} · {level}`, and under it, when that level is gapped, `{level} has its own Net: jack in there`; a plain site has none.
21. **Ruling:** Words: `FIREWALL · 1 lock` and `FIREWALL · 3 locks` (tower edge); `FIREWALL · 3 locks · 1/3 broken` (the gate refusing); `FIREWALL ▮▮▮ 0/3` (panel); `FIREWALL ▯▮▮ · 1/3 broken` (the city's card); `FIREWALL · 3 LOCKS` (HELIX); `8F–11F passed`, `8F passed` (slab). One glyph per lock in lock order: `▯` broken, `▮` whole.
22. **Ruling:** The tower: a folded level shows its nodes and ICE by their floor y against the first row of its second band (its middle row with one band): before it row 0, on it row 1, after it row 2; collisions shift right. With G folded its locks stand on the firewall's sides beside G's fold rows (lock 0 left row 1, 1 right row 1, 2 left row 0, 3 right row 0); open, they are G's wall nodes. On a site's Net the map's overlays that draw pieces by tile (ghost steps, the watch) take only the open level's.
23. **Ruling:** On a site's Net the cord is the shortest path over the open level from the node you jacked in at to you, drawn when both are on it.
24. **Ruling:** The DEVELOPER menu gets a SITE NET row: NEW GAME (DEBUG)'s start, then `venues::dev_site_net(st, SITE_NET_SECURITY)`: the type that builds `BuildingKind::Office`, MEDIUM, its first objective in a `Server`-kind room, security 190, the tick as seed, the mission copy begun, you on the street at its main door.
25. **Ruling:** Besides the two chapters the spec names, the HELIX chapter (`09-the-rig-and-helix.md`) and the `< >` row of the Controls chapter and `help.rs`'s `WALK` change, since a player sees them.
26. **Ruling (trace):** A run on a firewall lock makes the noise of a turn behind a gate (`Scene::Inside`'s), not the open grid's louder `owners::outside_noise`: the lock is the gate. A crack is 3 turns at noise 4, so a lock adds 12 heat; 3 locks 36 and 4 locks 48 against the alarm's 60, which leaves room for the work inside while every lock is still heard. A miss still adds its 2 more.
