The overworld in motion · round 2

Round 1's picks put together on one screen. What changed from round 1:

  • Neon (B) and weather (C) run together, with D's beacons at the places. Traffic (A), the highway lamps and their wave, and D's running dusk clock are gone: the roads stay still.
  • No rain. The cloud shadows stay.
  • New: every 40 to 70 seconds a gust runs through the hills and mountains, about five seconds across, then quiet. gust now sends one without waiting.
  • The neon is slower: a district takes 20 to 36 seconds per breath, where round 1 took 5 to 12. Sign stutters, window changes and tower blinks are much rarer (rates at the bottom).
  • The Net overworld stays as it is today. Its tab is only for reference.

What the Rust side would need

shared · the clock's light
  • atlas_cell takes the minute: lighting::daylight(minute) and lighting::sky(Street, minute), as the street already uses them. Each kind of tile gets a brightness (ground 0.4→1, water 0.55→1, settlements 0.72→1, open road 0.5→1, settlement road 0.82→1, places 0.85→1), and ground and water also take the dusk cast at that brightness. By day the look is exactly today's.
  • draw_world passes st.tick down the way draw_net_world already does. The screen already redraws every frame, and the window repaints only the cells that changed.
neon
  • Each district breathes on its own period with anim::effects::pulse, quantised to five steps, with a phase from its theme plus up to 60 ticks per tile from roll, so each step ripples through the district rather than landing all at once (a district id would let two districts of one theme breathe apart). Signs stay where today's look puts them (roll > 0.92), plus more after dark (0.86–0.92).
  • Beacons sit on each settlement's densest tiles, picked once and spaced five apart. The night spill uses each tile's distance (≤3) to the nearest urban tile and that tile's accent, computed once per world.
  • Everything is stateless, from the tick and hash(x, y):
    breathingperiod 600–1080 ticks (20–36 s) by district: CorpCore 720, NeonStrip 600, NightMarket 660, Docks 840, OldTown 900, Megablocks 960, Industrial 1080; five steps, so a district changes a step every 2–3.6 s
    sign stutterhalf the signs stutter; each one once every 1800–2700 ticks (60–90 s, hashed), for 12 ticks (0.4 s). Round 1: a third of the signs, every 5–10 s, for 16 ticks
    windowsdusk and night only; each dense tile redraws its light every 1800 ticks (60 s, staggered), lit 4 times in 5. Round 1: every 8 s, lit 7 in 10
    tower blink6 ticks on in every 180 (once every 6 s), each tower on its own phase. Round 1: 5 in 54 (every 1.8 s)
    water reflectionsafter dark, within 3 of a settlement; each tile has a 1-in-25 chance every 30 ticks (1 s). Round 1: 6% every 8 ticks
weather (no rain)
  • Swells come from hash(⌊(x − t/30)/3⌋, y) over the deep sea, each row stepping on its own hashed tick, so the whole sea never moves in one frame. A shore tile (already found in ground_cell) breaks when a swell is one tile out to sea.
  • The river needs its tiles in flow order. rivers.rs has each river's path while it generates: keep it on Overworld, or trace the link masks once from source to mouth.
  • Clouds are two octaves of value noise on the tile grid at three levels of shade.
  • The gust: a schedule of start ticks hashed from the world's seed, and a front f = 44 + (t − start)/3. A hill, mountain or lake tile is in the band when 0 ≤ f − (x + 0.6·(y − 16)) < 5, at strengths 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.3, 0.15 back from the front. Mountains whiten toward (235, 240, 250) (spindrift), hills take dust toward (205, 175, 125), lake ≈ turns to ~. Only the tile's kind is needed: Ground::Hills | Mountains | Peak and Water::Lake.
    gustevery 1200–2100 ticks (40–70 s, hashed); the front moves a tile per 3 ticks and crosses the range in about 230 ticks (7.7 s), so any one tile is in the band for about 0.5 s; 5-tile band, west to east like the clouds
    swellsa tile per 30 ticks (1 s) toward the coast
    river glintsa tile per 10 ticks downstream; a glint on 3 in 10 of the slots
    wind in the fieldsa 3-tile band every 34, a tile per 6 ticks
    cloud shadowsa tile per 45 ticks (1.5 s) east, a tile per 300 ticks north; each row and column steps on its own hashed tick
    sea glintsdusk and night; a 1-in-40 chance per tile every 7 ticks, amber at dusk, the moon's grey at night

NET
Nothing to build: the Net overworld keeps today's NetAtlas, with its one bright cell every 23 tiles along the open trunks.
cost
With all three layers on, about 24 cells change per tick by day and 33 at night, and no frame changes more than about 130. The swells and cloud steps account for about 20 of those; the neon adds 5 to 10 and the beacons 1 or 2. A gust adds about 5 while it runs. Everything is decided per cell, with nothing done per pixel.
